The Kingdom in the Sky

Completely surrounded by South Africa, the Kingdom of Lesotho is a land of dramatic highlands, deep river valleys, and an enduring independent spirit.

Known affectionately as "The Kingdom in the Sky", Lesotho holds the unique geographical distinction of having the highest lowest point of any nation in the world—every square meter rests at an elevation exceeding 1,000 meters (3,280 ft) above sea level, reaching its crown at Thabana Ntlenyana (3,482 m).

Founded in the early 19th century by the visionary diplomat and King Moshoeshoe I, the Basotho people successfully defended their independence from atop impregnable plateaus like Thaba Bosiu. Today, Lesotho welcomes world adventurers with pristine alpine air, warm hospitality, and timeless mountain traditions.

Fast Facts

Lesotho at a Glance

Essential geographic, cultural, and travel metrics for your visit.

Capital City

Maseru is the bustling capital, situated along the Mohokare (Caledon) River bordering South Africa.

Currency

Lesotho Loti (LSL / M), pegged 1:1 to the South African Rand (ZAR). Both currencies are widely accepted.

Official Languages

Sesotho and English. English is widely spoken in commerce, hotels, and tourist centers.

Highest Elevation

Thabana Ntlenyana (3,482 m) is the highest point in all of Southern Africa, south of Kilimanjaro.

Alpine Climate

Over 300 sunny days per year. Crisp temperate summers (Nov–Feb) and snow-capped winter wonderland (Jun–Aug).

National Motto

"Khotso, Pula, Nala": Peace, Rain, Prosperity. The foundational greeting of all Basotho people.

Heritage & Symbols

Cultural Emblems of the Basotho

Symbols that embody the resilience, artistry, and identity of the nation.

The Basotho Blanket (Seanamarena)

Worn with immense pride, the wool blanket is an iconic national garment. Distinct patterns mark royal events, childbirth, weddings, and traditional rites of passage into adulthood.

National Garment

The Mokorotlo Hat

Woven by hand from local mosea grass, this conical hat mirrors the shape of the sacred mountain Mount Qiloane. It is emblazoned on the Lesotho flag and vehicle license plates.

Flag Symbol

The Basotho Pony

Bred for sure-footed stamina across steep mountain shale and thin alpine air, the pony remains essential for highland transport and guided wilderness trekking.

Frequently Asked Questions

Helpful insights to prepare for your journey to Lesotho.

Citizens of many countries (including the UK, USA, EU nations, and SADC members) can enter Lesotho visa-free for stays up to 14 or 30 days. An electronic visa (eVisa) is easily obtainable online for travelers requiring one. Ensure your passport is valid for at least 6 months with 2 blank pages.

Lesotho is a year-round destination:
  • Spring/Summer (Oct–Mar): Lush green mountains, full waterfalls, pony trekking, and wildflower blooms.
  • Autumn (Apr–May): Golden light, crisp sunny days, and perfect hiking temperatures.
  • Winter (Jun–Aug): Snow sports at Afriski, frozen waterfalls, and crackling lodge fires.

Paved national highways connect major towns like Maseru, Roma, and Katse Dam. However, reaching Sani Pass, Sehlabathebe, and deep mountain trails strictly requires a high-clearance 4x4. The border post at Sani Pass will turn away 2WD vehicles for safety.

Basotho people are remarkably welcoming. Always greet people first ("Dumela" or "Khotso") and politely ask for permission before taking photographs of individuals, herd boys, or village homesteads.
